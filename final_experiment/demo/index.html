Counterfactual Second Look

A classifier (C0) reads a chest X-ray. A diffusion model then edits the image until C0 changes its mind, at three strengths. A second model (C2) looks at the image, the edit and how the image changed to judge whether C0's first call can be trusted. Its score is shown next to C0's own confidence (MSP).

How to read this. Scores are percentiles among C0's calls of the same kind (all positive calls, or all negative calls): 0 is the least trusted, 100 the most. C2 is trained with class-balanced weights, so its raw output is a ranking, not a probability. MSP is the threshold variant: the probability of the class C0 predicted at its own threshold.

The edit. Each counterfactual is generated from the input with FastDiME and guided to a target C0 probability (low, mid, high strength). The diffusion reconstruction alone changes every pixel a little, and that part is shared by all three levels. "Guided edit" shows the stronger levels minus the weakest, which isolates what the extra push changes. "Change vs input" shows everything, reconstruction included.

Cases. Per C0 outcome, 3 cases where C2 trusts the call much more than MSP does, 3 where it trusts it much less, and 4 at random. Every score is out-of-fold: the case was in the test fold of the model that scored it. Attributes compare the image with its high-strength counterfactual, in SD units of the images.
